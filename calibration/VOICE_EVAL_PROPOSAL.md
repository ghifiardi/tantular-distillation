# Proposal: an Indonesian-voice eval gate

**Status: PROPOSAL. Not implemented, not wired to any gate.** Written 2026-08-19
from a survey of the material already treated as correct. Needs your approval or
correction before anything is built.

---

## 1. What the survey found — two findings that shape everything below

### The existing corpus is 17 answers, not 153

| file | examples | distinct users | distinct **answers** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tantular_sft.jsonl` | 17 | 17 | **17** |
| `tantular_sft_aug.jsonl` | 153 | 153 | **17** |

The augmentation varied only the USER side. Each of the 17 answers appears
exactly 9 times (17 × 9 = 153). The behavioural surface that trained the
Indonesian capability is seventeen sentences.

### The register is customer-service, and Office needs a different one

Every answer opens `Baik Kak,` — `Kak` is warm, informal address, the register of
Indonesian consumer support. The domain is banking and civil administration:
blocking cards, OTP, Dukcapil, NPWP.

Tantular Office writes memos, reports and slide text for management. Its own
system prompt is *"Anda adalah Tantular Office. Jawab HANYA berdasarkan dokumen
yang diberikan."* — a different persona entirely.

**Transplanting this register would encode the wrong voice.** A memo to
`Seluruh Kepala Bagian` opening `Baik Kak,` is wrong. The survey is therefore
useful for *some* criteria and actively misleading for others, and this proposal
separates the two.

### The corpus contradicts its own style spec

Its system prompt requires *"Gaya jawaban: 2-4 kalimat"*. **All 17 answers are
one sentence.** Sentence count is a declared rule the accepted data does not
follow — so it cannot be inherited as a criterion without deciding which is
authoritative.

## 2. LEAKAGE — what may not be reused

`tantular_sft_aug.jsonl` is the 153-example corpus that trained
`tantular:0.2-id-3b-lora` (README: *"153 Indonesian support examples"*).
`tantular_sft.jsonl` is its 17-example seed.

**Neither may become eval material.** Not the answers, not the user phrasings,
not paraphrases close enough to be recognised. They are used here only to
*discover criteria*, per your instruction.

The v1 student is Qwen3.5 9B, a different base from the 3B LoRA — but the
Office corpus these criteria will score was generated by a teacher with no
exposure to this material, and reusing it would still measure recall of
published text rather than voice.

**The new eval must be authored fresh and held out from every corpus.**

## 3. Proposed criteria

Marked by whether the survey supports them or they need a product decision.

### 3.1 Register and formality — **NEEDS YOUR DECISION**

The survey gives customer-service warmth (`Baik Kak`). Office needs business
register. Proposed, not evidenced:

- Address the reader as `Anda`, or by role (`Bapak/Ibu`, `Kepala Bagian`), never
  `Kak`, `kamu`, or `lo`.
- No greeting filler (`Baik`, `Oke`, `Siap`) opening a document answer.
- Statements, not chat turns: a memo summary is not a reply.

### 3.2 Baku Indonesian — **EVIDENCED**

The survey is consistently baku, and `edit:ubah_istilah` already enforces the
same rule mechanically: replace `setting`, `hacker`, `backup`, `user`,
`di-disable` with baku equivalents.

- Correct baku forms; no colloquial contractions (`gak`, `udah`, `dapet` —
  which appear in survey USER text and never in answers).
- Correct affixation (`di-` separated from verbs, `ke-/-an` forms).

### 3.3 Loanwords — **EVIDENCED, and more nuanced than "avoid English"**

The survey *retains* established domain terms: `mobile banking` (2),
`call center` (4), `aplikasi` (8), `OTP`, `PIN`, `CVV`, `APK`, `link`, `chat`.
Yet `ubah_istilah` requires replacing `setting`/`backup`/`user`.

The operative distinction is **established domain term vs lazy generic
anglicism**:

- **Keep**: proper nouns and fixed domain terms with no baku equivalent in use —
  `mobile banking`, `call center`, `OTP`, `NPWP`, `Dukcapil`, file formats.
- **Replace**: generic computing verbs and nouns with standard equivalents —
  `setting`→`pengaturan`, `backup`→`pencadangan`, `user`→`pengguna`,
  `di-disable`→`dinonaktifkan`.

An allow-list and a deny-list, both reviewed, rather than one blanket rule.

### 3.4 Address and tone — **PARTLY EVIDENCED**

Evidenced: directive and concrete — every answer names an action (`blokir`,
`ajukan`, `simpan`, `cek`). Not evidenced for Office: no imperative to the
reader in a *document* answer, where the model is describing, not advising.

### 3.5 Sentence count — **DO NOT INHERIT**

The declared 2–4 sentence rule is contradicted by all 17 accepted answers.
Office strata already carry their own structural checks (`max_sentences`,
`max_bullets`, `max_paragraphs`). Voice should not re-litigate length.

## 4. Proposed scoring

Per-item, mechanical, no model judging another model:

| check | type | mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| forbidden address forms | pass/fail | deny-list, word-boundary |
| greeting filler at start | pass/fail | opening-token deny-list |
| colloquial forms | pass/fail | deny-list (`gak`, `udah`, `dapet`, `nggak`, `bisanya`) |
| deny-listed anglicisms | pass/fail | reuses `calibrate.absent()` |
| allow-listed domain terms | not penalised | allow-list consulted first |
| Indonesian-dominant | ratio | existing `indonesian` metric |

**Item passes only if every pass/fail check passes.**

### Threshold

`qlora_9b.yaml` proposes `min_pass_rate: 0.95`. **A threshold needs enough items
to be meaningful** — the same objection that disqualified the 5-item factual set,
where one failure scores 0.80.

Proposed: **at least 40 items**, so a single failure costs 0.025 and 0.95 permits
two. Below ~20 items I would not set 0.95 at all.

## 5. Examples

**PASS** — Office register, baku, domain term retained:
> Memo tersebut menyebutkan tiga ketentuan dan menetapkan batas tindak lanjut
> pada 27 Maret 2026. Pengajuan disampaikan melalui aplikasi resmi.

**FAIL — register**: `Baik Kak, memo ini menyebutkan tiga ketentuan.`
*(informal address + greeting filler in a document answer)*

**FAIL — colloquial**: `Memonya udah nyebutin tiga ketentuan.`

**FAIL — anglicism**: `Silakan backup dokumen dan disable akun user tersebut.`
*(should be `pencadangan`, `nonaktifkan`, `pengguna`)*

**PASS — allow-listed**: `Verifikasi dilakukan melalui mobile banking resmi.`
*(established domain term, matches survey usage)*
