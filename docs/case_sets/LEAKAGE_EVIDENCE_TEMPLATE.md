# Leakage evidence record — TEMPLATE

FIXTURE DATA. Binds to no shipped case set. `src/verify_case_set_leakage_evidence.py`
refuses it by name.

The **machine section** is generated by `src/check_case_leakage.py` and must be
pasted verbatim — it binds the exact bytes that were checked. The **human
section** is a person's judgement about what the check does and does not cover.

An agent may generate the machine section. It may **not** fill the reviewer
identity, and it may **not** turn `unverifiable` into `clean` — a component
nobody could compare was not found clean, and recording it as clean would make
the gate strongest exactly where it knows least.

## What the reviewer is accepting

`limitations_understood` asserts they have read that exact identity under
normalization v1 finds **exact** matches only — no paraphrase, no translation,
no lightly-edited document — and that a clean result means "no identical
normalized component was found", never "no related material exists".

`unverifiable_components_accepted` asserts they know **which** components could
not be checked and why. Today `request`, `document`, `expected_target`,
`expected_outcome` and `full_office_case` are all unverifiable: the promoted
corpora carry no document field, and no versioned extractor exists to split a
prompt into instruction and document. **A clean `user_payload` therefore says
nothing about a reused document inside a rephrased prompt.**

For the final Office held-out set, accepting that is not enough: document and
full-case coverage stay **blocked** until those components are genuinely
available or a human-approved extractor exists.

## Why it binds five digests

Case set, public manifest, private manifest, source bytes, and the checker's
own implementation. Change any one and the evidence stops matching, so a record
can never describe a check of something else. The source digest is the one that
matters most: **a changed corpus voids every earlier conclusion**, because they
were about different bytes.
